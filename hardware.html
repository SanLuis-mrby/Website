Hardware Problems

Hardware is the physical part of the computer that you can touch: the case, screen, keyboard, mouse, memory, hard drive, and cables. Hardware problems often show up suddenly, such as a computer that will not start or a screen that stays black.

Computer Will Not Turn On

Possible causes

  • The power cable is loose or the outlet is not working.
  • The laptop battery is empty or faulty.
  • The power supply unit (PSU) has failed.
  • The power button or motherboard is damaged.

What to do

  1. Check that the power cable is firmly connected at both ends.
  2. Plug another device into the same outlet to test it.
  3. If using a power strip, plug the computer directly into the wall.
  4. For a laptop, unplug the charger, remove the battery if possible, and hold the power button for 15 seconds. Reconnect and try again.
  5. Look for any lights or fan sounds. If there are none, the power supply may need replacing.

No Display (Black Screen)

Possible causes

  • The monitor is off or set to the wrong input.
  • The video cable (HDMI, VGA, DisplayPort) is loose.
  • The graphics card or memory is not seated properly.

What to do

  1. Make sure the monitor is turned on and its power light is lit.
  2. Reconnect the video cable on both the monitor and the computer.
  3. Press the monitor's input or source button and select the correct input.
  4. Try a different cable or a different monitor.
  5. If the computer beeps when starting, count the beeps. They are error codes for problems like memory failure.

Overheating and Loud Fan

Computers produce heat. Fans pull air through the case to cool the processor. When dust blocks the vents, the fan spins faster and louder, and the computer may slow down or shut off to protect itself.

  • Place a laptop on a hard, flat surface, not on a bed or pillow.
  • Clean the vents with compressed air. Hold the fan still while spraying.
  • Close programs that use a lot of processing power.
  • Make sure a desktop has space around it for airflow.
  • If it still overheats, the thermal paste may need replacing by a technician.

Keyboard or Mouse Not Working

  • Wired: Unplug and plug into a different USB port.
  • Wireless: Replace the batteries, check that the receiver is plugged in, and turn the device off and on.
  • Bluetooth: Remove the device in Bluetooth settings and pair it again.
  • Test the device on another computer to see if the device itself is broken.
  • On a laptop, the touchpad may be disabled. Look for a function key (Fn + F-key) with a touchpad icon.

USB Device Not Recognized

  1. Try a different USB port, preferably one on the back of a desktop.
  2. Try a different cable.
  3. Restart the computer with the device unplugged, then plug it in again.
  4. Open Device Manager and look for a yellow warning icon next to the device. Right-click it and choose Update driver.
  5. Test the device on another computer.

Strange Noises

Sound Possible Cause Action
Loud whirring Fan working hard because of dust or heat Clean the vents
Clicking or grinding Failing hard disk drive Back up files immediately and replace the drive
Repeated beeping at startup Memory or graphics problem Reseat the RAM and graphics card
Buzzing Loose part or failing power supply Check inside the case or see a technician

Warning: A clicking hard drive is a serious sign. Copy your important files to another drive right away and stop using the computer as much as possible.
