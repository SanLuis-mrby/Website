Security Problems

Computer security protects your files, accounts, and personal information from harmful software and people. A security problem can also cause slow performance, strange pop-ups, and other issues that look like ordinary computer faults.

Types of Threats

Threat What It Does
Virus Attaches itself to files and spreads when they are opened or shared
Malware General term for any harmful software
Adware Shows unwanted ads and pop-ups
Spyware Secretly collects information such as passwords
Ransomware Locks your files and demands payment to unlock them
Phishing Fake emails or websites that trick you into giving personal information

Signs Your Computer May Be Infected

  • The computer suddenly becomes very slow.
  • Pop-up ads appear even when no browser is open.
  • Your browser home page or search engine changed by itself.
  • Unknown programs appear in your program list.
  • Your antivirus is turned off and will not turn back on.
  • Friends receive strange messages from your accounts.
  • Files are missing or have strange new extensions.

What to Do If You Think You Have a Virus

  1. Disconnect from the internet to stop the threat from spreading or sending data.
  2. Start the computer in Safe Mode with Networking.
  3. Run a full scan with Windows Security or your antivirus program.
  4. Delete or quarantine anything the scan finds.
  5. Uninstall programs you do not recognize.
  6. Reset your browser settings and remove unknown extensions.
  7. Change your important passwords from a clean device.
  8. If the problem remains, back up your files and reinstall Windows.

Forgotten Password

  • For a Microsoft account, use the reset page on the sign-in screen and verify through your email or phone.
  • For a local account, use a password reset disk or security questions if you set them up.
  • For websites, use the Forgot password link and check your email, including the spam folder.
  • After resetting, use a unique password for each account and consider a password manager.

How to Spot a Phishing Email

  • It creates urgency, such as "Your account will be closed in 24 hours."
  • The sender's email address does not match the real company.
  • It contains spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • It asks for your password or bank details.
  • The link shown looks different when you hover over it.

Remember: Real companies never ask for your password by email. When in doubt, do not click the link. Go to the official website by typing the address yourself.

Good Security Habits

  • Keep Windows, your browser, and your programs updated.
  • Use Windows Security or another trusted antivirus program.
  • Use strong passwords with at least 12 characters, mixing letters, numbers, and symbols.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for important accounts.
  • Download programs only from official websites and stores.
  • Do not open attachments from unknown senders.
  • Keep regular backups so you can recover from ransomware.
